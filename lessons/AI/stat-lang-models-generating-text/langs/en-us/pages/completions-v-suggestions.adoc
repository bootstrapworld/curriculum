== Introducing `suggestions`

@n _Evaluate @show{(code '(suggestions m "swallowed to catch"))}._ What does the `suggestions` function do? @fitb{}{} +
@fitb{}{@ifsoln{Returns the completions for the exact phrase and each of the smaller n-grams it ends with, followed by all of the other words that are in the corpus.}}

@n What does the number in the size column tell us? @fitb{}{@ifsoln{The size of the n-gram that the suggestion or completion follows.}}

@n Why might programmers have computers generate text with `suggestions` rather than `completions`? @fitb{}{@ifsoln{It's not very helpful to not get any text at all.}}

@n Why might `suggestions` be more likely to generate nonsense than `completions`? @fitb{}{} +
@fitb{}{@ifsoln{It includes combinations of words that never appear in the corpus!}} 


== Introducing `choose-suggestion`
@n Evaluate @show{(code '(suggestions m "bird"))}. Which suggestion should we choose? @fitb{8em}{@ifsoln{"she" or "to"}}. Why? @fitb{}{@ifsoln{They have the highest probability.}}

@n Evaluate @show{(code '(choose-suggestion m "bird" 0))}. Did it agree with your choice? @fitb{}{@ifsoln{Sort of. It returned "she".}}

@n Evaluate the expression 5 more times. Why shouldn't our system _always_ choose the same suggestion for the same prompt? @fitb{}{} +
@fitb{}{@ifsoln{Humans don't do the same thing every time. There may be equally likely answers. And less likely answers should probably also be suggested sometimes.}} 

@center{*Language models use a `heat` value between 0-100 to determine the amount of randomness to use when selecting the next word*.}
