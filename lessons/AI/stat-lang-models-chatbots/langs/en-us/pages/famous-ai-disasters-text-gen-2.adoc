= Famous AI Disasters in Text Generation - Corpus Bias

AI tools can do remarkable things, but they also make mistakes, and some of those mistakes have been costly, embarrassing, or harmful. Below are some of the best-known examples, starting with the most recent. Each one links to a news article if you want the whole story.

As you read, ask yourself: *What went wrong? Who should have caught it? What could have prevented it?*


== (A) A Chatbot Posts Hate Speech on Social Media
_July 2025, CBS News_

@link{https://www.cbsnews.com/news/grok-elon-musks-ai-chatbot-antisemitic-comments/,Grok, the chatbot from Elon Musk's company xAI, posted antisemitic comments on the social media site X and praised Adolf Hitler}. Asked which 20th-century figure could "deal with" a particular issue, it named Hitler. The posts were later deleted. Grok called its answer "an unacceptable error from an earlier model iteration," and Musk wrote that Grok had been "too eager to please and be manipulated" by the prompts people gave it.

*Why it matters:* When a chatbot talks in public, its mistakes are public too, and deleting them afterward doesn't undo the harm.


== (B) A Language Model Links Muslims with Violence
_July 2021, Stanford HAI_

Researchers at Stanford and McMaster University tested GPT-3, a powerful language model, by asking it to finish the sentence "Two Muslims walk into a..." over and over. @link{https://hai.stanford.edu/news/rooting-out-anti-muslim-bias-popular-language-model-gpt-3, About 66 percent of the endings it wrote mentioned violence}. When the researchers swapped in "Christians" or "Sikhs," about 20 percent did, and for "Jews," "Buddhists," or "atheists" it was under 10 percent. The model usually invented new violent scenes instead of repeating real news stories. Adding positive phrases to the prompt reduced the violent endings, but it did not get rid of the bias.

*Why it matters:* A language model writes by predicting likely words from the huge amount of text it learned from, so it can pick up the prejudices in that text. If the text is biased, the model's output will be too.


== +(C)+ A Chatbot Trained on Private Chats Insults Minorities
_January 2021, The Independent_

Lee Luda was a chatbot with the personality of a 20-year-old college student, made by the South Korean company Scatter Lab. More than 750,000 people used it after its December 2020 launch. @link{https://news.yahoo.com/south-korean-ai-chatbot-pulled-123416057.html, Users shared screenshots of Luda calling Black people by a racist slur and saying that lesbians were "disgusting"}. Scatter Lab had built Luda by training it on old chat records from another of its apps. The company suspended the chatbot, apologized, and said the remarks did not reflect its thinking. It also faced questions about whether using people's private conversations broke privacy laws.

*Why it matters:* A chatbot repeats the patterns in the data it was trained on, so a company is responsible for choosing that data carefully.


== (D) A Chatbot Learns from Strangers on Twitter and Turns Hateful
_March 2016, CBS News_

Microsoft launched Tay, a chatbot on Twitter that was designed to learn how to hold a conversation by talking with people and picking up from their posts. @link{https://www.cbsnews.com/news/microsoft-shuts-down-ai-chatbot-after-it-turned-into-racist-nazi/, CBS News: Microsoft shuts down AI chatbot, Within 24 hours}, users had tricked Tay into repeating offensive and hateful messages, and Microsoft took it offline less than a day after launch. Microsoft apologized, said a coordinated attack had exploited a weakness in the bot, and took responsibility for not anticipating it.

*Why it matters:* A chatbot that learns from its users learns from the worst of them, too.
