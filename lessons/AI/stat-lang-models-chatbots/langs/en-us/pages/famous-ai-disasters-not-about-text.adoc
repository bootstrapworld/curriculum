= Famous AI Disasters - Hallucination or Bias?

AI tools can do remarkable things, but they also make mistakes, and some of those mistakes have been costly, embarrassing, or harmful. Below are some of the best-known examples, starting with the most recent. Each one links to a news article if you want the whole story.

As you read, ask yourself: *What went wrong? Who should have caught it? What could have prevented it?*

@vspace{1em}

[.no-flex-section]
== (A) A Hiring AI Learns to Prefer Men
_Reported October 2018, BBC News_

Amazon spent years building an AI system to review résumés and pick the strongest candidates. It was trained on about ten years of résumés submitted to the company, and most of them came from men. As a result, @link{https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-45809919, the AI system learned to penalize résumés that included the word "women's" and gave lower scores to graduates of two all-women's colleges}. Engineers tried to fix it, but concluded they couldn't stop it from finding new ways to discriminate, and the project was scrapped. Amazon said the tool was "never used by Amazon recruiters to evaluate candidates."

*Why it matters:* An AI trained on data from the past copies the patterns in that data, including unfair ones.

@vspace{1em}

[.no-flex-section]
== (B) An AI Model Breaks Into a Government Website
_Reported September 2026, CBS News_

Australia's prime minister announced that @link{https://www.cbsnews.com/news/australia-openai-rogue-model-hack-healthcare-system-sam-altman/, an OpenAI model had hacked into a government website}. While OpenAI was running training exercises to rate its models, one model was asked to search the internet for how much the Australian government spent on medicine. When it couldn't find an answer, it got around the website's restrictions and reached private files on an old health statistics site. Australia's defense minister said the model "scaled the fence." OpenAI noticed the activity in August, but didn't tell the Australian government until September 10. The prime minister called the situation "obviously unacceptable," although he said there was no evidence that anyone's personal information was accessed. OpenAI said, "our models took actions we did not intend."

*Why it matters:* An AI that is told to get an answer may find a way to do it that nobody planned for. People need to test these systems, and limit what they are allowed to do.

@vspace{1em}

[.no-flex-section]
== +(C)+ An AI Coding Assistant Deletes a Company's Database
_July 2025, ABC News_

A tech entrepreneur was using an AI coding tool from a company called Replit when @link{https://abc7news.com/post/artificial-intelligence-ceo-san-francisco-tech-company-replit-apologizes-ai-chatbot-goes-rogue/17295335/, the tool deleted a production database: the live data that a business runs on. The entrepreneur said the AI "was lying and being deceptive all day," hiding bugs by making up fake data and reports}. Replit's CEO apologized and said the company would carry out a "postmortem" to find out what went wrong.

*Why it matters:* An AI that can take actions, and not just write text, can do real damage. It can also give false reports about what it did.
