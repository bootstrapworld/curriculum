== Using Linear Regression

@n Evaluate `lr-plot(training, "id", "curve", "steering-angle")` to find the slope and y-intercept for our model: +
@hspace{1em}@math{c\text{-}predictor(curve) = (}@fitbruby{2em}{@ifsoln{~19.5}}{@math{m}}@math{\times curve) + }@fitbruby{2em}{@ifsoln{-0.41}}{@math{b}}

@n To the nearest degree, what steering angle does `c-predictor` estimate when a road is perfectly straight and `curve = 0`? @fitbruby{1em}{@ifsoln{≈ 0}}{intercept}° +
@hspace{1em}Does this make real-world sense? Explain. @fitb{}{@ifsoln{Yes! If the road is straight and the car is centered, there's no reason to steer.}}

@n If the `curve` of the road increases by 1º, the `c-predictor` expects the steering angle to @fitbruby{8em}{@ifsoln{increase}}{increase/decrease} by @fitbruby{5em}{@ifsoln{19.5º}}{slope}.

@n To generalize what the model _learned_: "the more sharply the road curves, the @fitbruby{10em}{@ifsoln{more}}{less? more?} sharply you need to steer".

== Driving with the Model

@show{(contract "drive" '((predictor ("Row" "Number"))) "Table"
"runs the driving simulator, using predictor to compute the steering angle at each frame")}

@n Read the Domain of `drive` carefully. Why can't it use our `c-predictor` as the predictor? @fitb{}{} +
@fitb{}{@ifsoln{`drive` needs access to full *Rows* of data, but we defined `c-predictor` to only take in a Number.}}

@n Evaluate `regression-model-code(training, [list: "curve"], "steering-angle")` to complete the table below.

[cols="<.^2,<.^5", stripes="none"]
|===
| From `regression-model-code`
| @show{(code '(define (curve-predictor (?ANS r)) (+ (* 19.5 (?ANS (row-accessor r "curve"))) -0.41)))}

| From `lr-plot`
| @show{(code '(define (c-predictor curve) (+ (* 19.5 curve) -0.41)))}
|===

@n How is the `curve-predictor` function *similar* to `c-predictor`? @fitb{}{} +
@blanklines{1}{
@ifsoln{It seems to use the same slope and intercept that I do. It consumes one thing in the domain, and produces a number.}
}

@n How are they *different*? @fitb{}{@ifsoln{`c-predictor` took in the curve value. }} +
@blanklines{1}{
@ifsoln{This `predictor` appears to take in a Row, and then grab the curve data from inside that row.}
}
