4+| Training means...

| Start with a multi-column dataset with at least one categorical column...
| Start with a multi-column dataset with at least one quantitative column...
| Start with a collection of items (e.g., photos, documents, audio samples)...
| Start with from a corpus of items (e.g., writings, audio samples, recipes)...

| ...build a decision tree in which a categorical variable is predicted from the others
| ...using regression (statistics) to compute slopes and y-intercepts that best fit a set of points
| ...map items to points and finding clusters of points that are near one another
| ...build a large "table" of what word most likely comes next

4+| After training...

| Given a new observation/row, follow its values down the tree branches until reach the predicted category
| Given a new observation/row, plug its values into y = b₀ + b₁x₁ + b₂x₂, + ... to get prediction for y
| Compute centroid of likes/dislikes, then return existing clusters whose centroids are closest ("most similar") to likes/dislikes one
| Given a prompt, lookup in "tables" to get next word, then repeat with the extended phrase, over and over

4+| Math you need...
| *Ratio & probability* to compute the accuracy of a prediction if we split on each variable, and *dot plots* to visualize distributions
| *Scatter plots* to visualize relationships, *line of best fit* to model them, *slope and y-intercept* to describe them
| *Average/mean* to compute centroids from a set of points, *pythagorean distance* or *angle measures* to determine how close/similar items are to those centroids
| *Conditional probability* to determine the most likely next-word to follow a starting phrase.
